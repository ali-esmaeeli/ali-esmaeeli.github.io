About

A chemical engineer turned water researcher, working to make earth a bluer planet.

I am a PhD student in the Water Environment Technology division at Chalmers, focusing on water quality monitoring for drinking water treatment. Our research moves water treatment towards online monitoring: I use the optical signature of organic matter to optimise treatment steps, with a particular focus on coagulation and on granular activated carbon (GAC) filters for PFAS removal.

Before Chalmers, I studied Environmental Engineering at the University of Tehran and Chemical Engineering at Iran University of Science and Technology, researched water reuse with the UNESCO Chair on Water Reuse, and worked as a process safety engineer in the oil and gas sector.

Research

Licentiate thesis · 2026

Proactive coagulant dosing

Climate change, hydrological variability and land use are making raw water less predictable than the plants treating it were designed for. I evaluated ARIX, an optical index of dissolved organic matter aromaticity that sensors can measure in real time, across a 240-km river system over a year. Reaching 70% DOC removal took about 2.5× more coagulant for low-ARIX water (~0.6) than high-ARIX water (~0.8). Even small shifts in organic matter quality change coagulant demand, which supports real-time dosing that follows the source water.

Since 2022 · Chalmers

GAC filters for PFAS removal

Part of the team behind a fluorescence-based sensor for granular activated carbon filters, from early design through testing to installation. Since March 2026 it has been running at a drinking water treatment plant, monitoring 10 carbon filters and their PFAS removal online.

MSc thesis · 2020 – 2022 · University of Tehran

Closed-loop water in pulp & paper

Master's thesis carried out with a pulp and paper mill to reduce its freshwater consumption. Using pinch analysis and mathematical optimisation, I designed three water networks, with savings ranging from 4% with minor changes to the plant up to 96% with a major redesign.
